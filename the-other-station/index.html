01 Research on China's space station, 2010 to 2026

The Other Station

In early November, scientists in China reported in Science that they had made the coldest atoms ever produced in space, aboard the Tiangong space station. The atoms got below 10 trillionths of a degree above absolute zero and held together for 7 seconds. NASA's lab on the International Space Station manages about one second. NASA plans to retire the ISS around 2030. If no commercial station is ready by then, Tiangong will be the only government lab in orbit.

02 Research papers that name each station, 2010 to 2026

About a sixth as many papers as the ISS

In 2025, 141 papers in the OpenAlex index named Tiangong or its modules in the title or abstract. 855 named the ISS. Tiangong's count has risen 74% since it was finished in 2022. From 2022 through 2026 so far, it comes to 591 papers against 3,836, about 15%.

2026 runs to early November. Papers about China's planned CSST space telescope, which will fly beside the station, are left out. Part of the ISS drop after 2020 is likely a change in how the index collects papers, not less research. By the agencies' own counts, China reported more than 230 papers from Tiangong in 2025, and NASA counted 361 ISS papers in its 2024 fiscal year. Hover or tap a year.

03 Tiangong papers for every 100 ISS papers, by field, 2022 to 2026

Strong in physics, nearly absent in biology

The gap depends on the field. In atomic and optical physics, where cold-atom work sits, Tiangong produced 43 papers for every 100 from the ISS. Materials, radiation and fluid physics run between 33 and 46. In human physiology and medicine, the ISS's biggest field, the figure is 5. Molecular biology, plant science and atmospheric science are almost empty.

04 Countries of co-authors on Tiangong papers, outside China, 2022 to 2026

Europe's next cosmic-ray detector is headed to Tiangong

Researchers in China co-wrote 439 of the 591 Tiangong papers. Italy is the largest foreign partner, on 35. Almost all of those describe one instrument: HERD, a large cosmic-ray detector being built with Italian, Swiss and Spanish groups to fly on Tiangong. Swiss and Polish groups are working on POLAR-2, a gamma-ray burst detector for the station. U.S. researchers appear on 11.

A paper counts once for each country among its authors' affiliations. U.S. law bars NASA from most direct cooperation with China's space program, but it doesn't stop university researchers from co-writing papers. Hover or tap a bar.

05 How long a cloud of ultracold atoms held together, in seconds

Where the coldest atoms in space are now made

Cold atoms behave like one big quantum wave. In free fall they can be held longer and cooled further than on the ground. That makes them useful for precise tests of gravity and for better clocks. NASA's Cold Atom Lab has led this work since 2018. The Tiangong team says its cloud of about 20,000 rubidium atoms lasted about 7 times as long.

Times and temperatures are as reported by the South China Morning Post and Science News from the Science paper. Groups measure temperature in different ways, so the comparison is rough. The ISS has reached effective temperatures as low as 52 trillionths of a degree. Tiangong reports below 10.

06 Method

How the papers were counted

Data

Paper counts come from OpenAlex, a free index of more than 250 million research works, queried November 9, 2026. A paper counts for the ISS when its title or abstract contains "International Space Station." It counts for Tiangong when its title or abstract names the China Space Station, the Tiangong station or its Tianhe, Wentian or Mengtian modules, or the Tiangong-1 and Tiangong-2 test labs. Papers that mention the CSST telescope, the Tianhe supercomputer or other survey telescopes are left out.

Fields are OpenAlex's subfield label for each paper's main topic. Countries come from author affiliations.

Limits

  • Naming a station isn't the same as using data from it. Some papers discuss a station without running an experiment on it.
  • OpenAlex covers English-language journals best. Papers in Chinese-language journals are undercounted, so Tiangong's figures are likely low.
  • OpenAlex changed sources in 2022 and lost some abstracts. Counts across that year are less comparable than counts within the same years.
  • China's paper and project totals are self-reported.
