AI data centers, 2021 to 2029

The Gigawatt Rush

The largest AI data centers can now draw about 18 gigawatts of power, 2.7 times as much as a year ago. Epoch AI tracks 94 of them from satellite images, permits and filings. The 77 in the United States light up here in the order they came online.

The plans

Fifteen U.S. sites of a gigawatt or more by 2029

Today two sites draw a gigawatt or more: Colossus 2 in Memphis and Amazon's New Carlisle campus in Indiana, built for Anthropic. Construction under way would put fifteen U.S. sites past that mark by January 2029, plus Stargate UAE in Abu Dhabi.

The scale

Almost half of America's nuclear fleet

Each symbol is one gigawatt of the 98 GW that U.S. nuclear plants can produce. The tracked U.S. AI sites need 15 GW today and 43 GW by 2029 if they are built as planned.

This compares capacity, the most power each can handle at once. Data centers rarely run at full capacity, while nuclear plants run close to it all year. U.S. nuclear figure: EIA, net summer capacity of 96 reactors, March 2026.

The speed

From bare ground to power in 109 days

For the 70 sites already running, Epoch dates the first sign of construction and the first power. The typical site took 2.2 years. Colossus 1 in Memphis took 109 days, inside a former Electrolux factory.

The builders

Stargate puts Oracle in first place

Oracle owns most of the Stargate sites that OpenAI will use. It has 0.7 GW today and 8.4 GW planned by 2029, more than any other owner. Epoch estimates the cost of all 94 sites will rise from $533 billion today to $1.42 trillion.

Method

How the numbers were built

Data

Epoch AI's Frontier Data Centers dataset, downloaded October 17, 2026. It covers 94 of the largest AI data centers, 77 of them in the United States. For each site Epoch builds a timeline of power capacity, chips and cost from satellite images, permits, utility filings and company statements.

Every total here adds each site's latest value on that date. Values after October 17, 2026 are Epoch's projections. Map positions are town-level, taken from each site's address.

Limits

  • Power is facility capacity, not the energy a site actually uses.
  • The dataset covers frontier-scale sites, not every data center.
  • Plans can slip or be canceled. Most future capacity has not been built.
  • Owners are as Epoch tags them. Some sites have no disclosed owner.
