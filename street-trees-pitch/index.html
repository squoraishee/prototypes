A pitch for The Pudding

One bug away

New York counted every street tree in 2015. Most neighborhoods lean heavily on one or two kinds of tree. Insects that kill trees pick specific kinds, so on some blocks, a single pest could take out a third of the shade.

    Almost every neighborhood breaks the 10 percent rule

    Urban foresters use a rule of thumb from Frank Santamour, published in 1990: no single species should make up more than 10 percent of a city's trees, no single genus more than 20 percent, and no single family more than 30 percent. The idea is that a pest or disease that wipes out one kind of tree should not take most of a neighborhood's shade with it.
