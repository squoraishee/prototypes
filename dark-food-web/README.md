# Who Eats Whom in the Dark

A scrolling visual essay about how deep-sea animals depend on one another, and on food falling from the sunlit surface.

**Live page:** https://squoraishee.github.io/dark-food-web/

## What's on the page

1. **The dive.** Scrolling takes you from the surface to the bottom of the Challenger Deep, about 10,900 meters down. A gauge tracks depth, pressure, sunlight, and temperature. Animated animals appear at their typical depths: lanternfish, a siphonophore, the barreleye, the vampire squid, a sperm whale hunting a giant squid, an anglerfish, the gulper eel, a dumbo octopus, sea pigs on the abyssal plain, a snailfish, and amphipods at the bottom.
2. **The nightly commute.** An interactive simulation of daily vertical migration. Lanternfish rise at night to eat zooplankton. Tuna catch them only when there is light to see. Droppings carry carbon below 400 meters at dawn, and a sperm whale dives to hunt squid. A button turns the migration off, so you can watch how many more lanternfish get caught when they stay shallow by day.
3. **When a whale falls.** An animated timeline of a whale carcass on the seafloor through its three stages: mobile scavengers, enrichment opportunists, and the long sulfophilic stage.

## Accuracy notes

- The creatures are drawn from their real features: the barreleye's transparent head and tubular eyes, the vampire squid's webbed cape and feeding filaments, the anglerfish's lure, the gulper eel's jaw and glowing tail tip. They are illustrations and not to scale with one another.
- Depths are typical ranges. Sunlight is modeled as falling to 1 percent of the surface value at 200 meters. Temperatures are typical and vary by ocean.
- The commute and whale fall animations simplify real processes. Their counts are not real numbers.
