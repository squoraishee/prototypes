Forest light

Reading the light under a forest

Leaves take sunlight apart, wavelength by wavelength. Follow the photons from the sun to the forest floor, then up to a satellite that reads a forest's health from orbit.

01

Sunlight is many colors

A prism sorts each photon by wavelength. The pile builds Planck's curve for the sun's 5,800 K surface.

02

A leaf is a filter

Chlorophyll absorbs blue and red. Green bounces back. Near infrared, invisible to us, mostly passes through or scatters back.

03

Light sinks through the canopy

Each layer of leaves catches more blue and red than far red. What reaches the floor is dim and shifted.

04

Reading the forest from orbit

A satellite compares near-infrared and red light. The ratio, NDVI, separates forest from crops, soil, and water.

05

What one spectrum cannot tell you

Shadows, wet soil, sun angle, and species mix all shift the numbers. A dense forest and a lush field can share an NDVI. Good analysis combines bands, dates, and ground checks.

Simulations built from typical sunlight, leaf, soil, and water spectra, not measurements of a real site. Planck's law, the Beer-Lambert law, NDVI, and the CIE 1931 color functions are standard. The curve shapes are simplified.
